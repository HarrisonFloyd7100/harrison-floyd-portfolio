Off the clock

My Top 3 Favorite Games.

Hades 1&2

What I love

This is the game that made me fall in love with roguelites. There's something so satisfying about the Sisyphean feel they create, and few games do it better than one that literally has Sisyphus as an ally. The unforgettable characters, the relationships you build with them, the beautiful world, the homage to Greek mythology: there's so much to love in this duology. But the two biggest standouts are the combat and the way the run structure drives the story.

The Combat. I could rant about the weapons for days. Every weapon has multiple aspects that change how it plays, so a single weapon can feel like several different ones. On top of that, the boon system means almost every modifier you pick up finds a use regardless of your build. Duo boons, keepsakes, and the Mirror upgrades all push you to experiment instead of settling on one "correct" loadout. It's a masterclass in gameplay mechanics, where depth comes from how the systems combine rather than from piling on more of them.

The Narrative. Making the run itself the way you progress characters and their relationships was truly innovative, and it never feels forced. I've never thought, "Now I have to go talk to this random guy Patroclus so I can get closer with Achilles." I think, "I really hope I run into Patroclus on this run so I can get his item and advance Achilles' story." Death isn't a setback, it's the thing that moves the story forward, so the repetition becomes the reason you keep playing.

Why It Matters to Me as a Developer. Supergiant took the best parts of the first game and did it all over again, and better, with the sequel. It's the benchmark I measure my own design thinking against: the mechanics, the narrative, and the pacing all reinforce each other instead of competing.

Titanfall 2

What I love

Titanfall 2 is without a doubt the greatest FPS shooter game ever created. There's never been an FPS quite like it and there probably never will be again.

As a fan of FPS shooters (I grew up playing every Call of Duty I could for as long as I could) the second I got my hands on Titanfall 2 I realized it was something else entirely. The gunplay was tighter and more responsive than anything I had ever felt. The movement (which will get its own paragraph here in just a second) is one of the greatest things I've ever felt in gaming and it instilled in me a love for a mechanic so deep I guarantee some mention of it will be found on every GDD and TDD I write till I retire. Then there's the campaign, oh man that campaign.

The Campaign. I consider myself somebody who really appreciates the campaigns in FPS games that are targeted towards a more multiplayer demographic. Titanfall 2's campaign is a complete step above every one of its competitors (eat your heart out BO2 and every Battlefield). The story of an underqualified rifleman being thrust into the mantle of responsibility alongside his 200 ton metal dog is just amazing. Even today after countless playthroughs I can't help but smile every single time BT speaks the words "Trust Me." This campaign has such a beautiful simple narrative that I'm going to treasure forever. Even more impressive is how these devs created their maps and their levels. Several times there will be an AMAZING mechanic that you could create an entire 20 hour long game around and the devs say "that was great, on to the next one." Effect and Cause's time travel was a stellar mindfreak, Into the Abyss where you have to navigate through a giant mech sized factory line without getting crushed, traversing between giant flying airships via robot fastballs and wall riding on the sides of planes in Trial by Fire, and so many more. Every level was so unique, and so interesting, and so challenging, and so rewarding. Even if you couldn't care less about the Militia and the fight for liberation, you loved the gameplay, and you couldn't help but become deeply invested in the friendship and the trust that Cooper and BT build as they navigate those challenges. It's beautiful every single time.

The Movement. What is there to say about Titanfall 2's movement that hasn't been said. It's tight, it's quick, you can get fast really quick, and you can get incredibly fast with a whole lot of practice. It's just oozing with skill expression, and every single other mechanic in the game becomes that much more satisfying once you get the movement down. You want to enter your mech? Sure you can walk up like a newbie and probably get killed or you can fly right into it at 100 mph because you got a run up using wall runs and get right into the action. Just died? You can take 5 minutes and run over to the fight or you can take 15 seconds and wall run, grappling hook and/or stim boost right over there. It's truly the core of the fun in Titanfall, and the player can feel just how much the devs know that in every level, weapon and perk. It makes the game incredibly fun literally all of the time you're playing it.

Celeste

What I love

This one will be a little shorter than the Titanfall 2 section. Celeste is one of those games where you hear about it every once in a while and think, “Oh yeah, that sounds interesting,” and then don't touch it for years. Once you do though, you realize just how wrong you were to wait.

The Presentation. Beautiful pixel art from head to toe, an immersive story, and a soundtrack that can bring tears to the eyes of even the most hardened man. There's so much personality packed into this game. Every part of the mountain has its own atmosphere, and the music makes the quiet moments feel just as important as the moments where you're desperately trying to stick a landing.

The Platforming. You can run, jump, climb, and dash. That sounds pretty simple until you see just how much the developers can do with those mechanics. A room can look completely impossible when you first enter it, and then a few attempts later you're flying through it wondering why you ever thought it was difficult. The controls are so precise that you start noticing exactly where you went wrong and exactly what you need to do differently. And when you finally pull it off? Oh man. There's something incredibly satisfying about making a sequence of jumps that would have seemed ridiculous to you ten minutes earlier.

The Design. What I really love from a design perspective is how little time the game wastes when you fail. You die, you're back, and you're already trying again. That quick reset makes you willing to experiment, take risks, and keep working at a room until it clicks. The challenge can be brutal, but the game gives you every opportunity to improve. It understands how much more willing a player is to attempt something difficult when another attempt is only a second away.

The Story. And that experience fits Madeline's story so beautifully. You're climbing this mountain with her, struggling with her, and slowly learning to move forward even when the next step feels impossible. Her anxiety and self-doubt give the climb a weight that goes far beyond reaching the top of a level. By the time you overcome a difficult section, you understand a little more of what that journey means to her.

The Whole Package. Celeste is a game I love for both the feeling of playing it and the thought behind its design. The movement, level design, music, and story all contribute to that same experience of struggling, learning, and eventually doing something you weren't sure you could do. It's beautiful, it's challenging, and it makes every hard-earned bit of progress feel incredible.
